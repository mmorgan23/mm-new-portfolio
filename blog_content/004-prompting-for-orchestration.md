## The Great AI Consolidation: Which Tools Actually Survive the Next 3 Years

Okay, real talk: the AI tool landscape right now is absolutely *insane*. Every time I open ProductHunt, there's another startup promising to revolutionize strategy, product development, legal compliance, sales—you name it. There's probably a dozen of them for each category.

But here's the thing nobody wants to admit out loud: most of them are gonna be gone in 3 years. Not because they're bad. Just because that's how this tends to work.

And honestly? I'm not being pessimistic here. This is just watching patterns repeat.

## The Consolidation Thesis

Here's what I think is actually happening over the next 3+ years:

The boring AI models—Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor—become the brain. The workhorses. The reasoning engines everyone plugs into. Meanwhile, the *actually valuable* tools—Salesforce, QuickBooks, Figma, GitHub—they become the data layer. The foundation.

And when you combine those two? That's where the real defensibility lives.

So what actually survives? Three types of tools:

**Tools that own your data.** Salesforce has your CRM locked in. QuickBooks has your financials. Carta owns your cap table. If you try to leave, it's painful. *Really* painful. That switching cost? That's what keeps them alive.

**Tools that know their domain cold.** Ironclad has learned from thousands of contracts. Your financial models are built on specific SaaS metrics that generic AI doesn't just *know*. Your legal work requires expertise. And when mistakes are expensive, you're gonna stick with what works.

**Tools that let you build with commodity AI + your own logic.** Your firm has a specific investment thesis. Your sales team has a unique process. Your operations run on workflows nobody else has. When you combine Claude with *your* proprietary stuff, you've built something that's actually hard to copy.

Everything else? The single-purpose AI copilots without real domain expertise? The tools that don't own data or expertise? The one-size-fits-all solutions trying to be everything to everyone? Those are the casualties waiting to happen.

## Why Generic "AI Copilots" Are Doomed (And It's Actually Pretty Simple)

Let me give you a concrete example: strategy work.

You can hire a consultant for $10k to run a strategic planning session. Or you could pay for some specialized strategy AI tool like Tome. But here's what's *actually* happening now:

You throw your data into Claude. Ask it to generate your strategic narrative. Copy-paste the output into PowerPoint. Present to investors. Done.

And suddenly Tome looks like an expensive middleman that you don't really need.

This is the pattern that's gonna repeat everywhere:

**Product specs?** Claude + Cursor handles it.

**Sales outreach?** Claude + Salesforce.

**Legal templates?** Claude + Ironclad (you still want domain expertise to validate it).

The UI was never the value. The value was always the reasoning. And now that everyone's got access to the same reasoning engines, the UI is basically just... nice to have.

## What This Actually Means for You

If you're building an AI tool right now, you gotta ask yourself some hard questions:

*Does my tool own data that's expensive for people to move?* Does it bake in domain expertise that competitors can't just duplicate? Or... am I building a prettier interface on top of Claude?

If it's that third one? Your runway is ticking down faster than you think.

But here's the flip side—and this is where it gets interesting:

**If you're a founder or operator, the real opportunity is inverted.** Your moat doesn't come from having the latest AI. It comes from proprietary logic and data that actually matters.

A VC fund that combines Claude with its own relationship intelligence engine—the network map of who-knows-who—that's genuinely unique. A SaaS company that merges Claude with domain models so good that customers in that industry just *recognize them as correct*? That builds real defensibility.

But a generic "oh we added AI to our product" feature? That's not a moat. That's a commodity. And commodities compete on price.

## The Real Opportunity Is Here Now

The companies that're gonna win the next 3 years aren't the specialized AI tool vendors. It's gonna be the ones that stack three things together:

- **Existing data locks** (Salesforce, QuickBooks, GitHub)
- **Domain expertise** (your industry knowledge, your models, your networks)
- **Commodity reasoning engines** (Claude, Cursor—the stuff everyone can access)

That combination? That's actually hard to replicate.

The future isn't about finding the "best AI tool." It's about orchestrating AI *with* your proprietary data and logic in ways that matter specifically to your business.

The consolidation isn't coming. Honestly? It's already happening. We're just living through it.
